# Over-promise patterns - leads, with floors and bounded rewrites

Each row is a **lead**, not a verdict. Before it becomes a finding, three things have to hold. The
floor has to be verified in `law.md` for a regime that applies (`audit.md` Phase 2). The clause has
to be read in context, with its qualifiers. And the exposure has to be checked against the inventory.
The rewrites are starting points. Adapt them to the document's voice, and re-audit them (Phase 6,
R5/R6).

Rewrite rules, for every row:

- **At the floor, never under it.** If a law requires a statement, keep it, and keep it as specific as
  that law demands (a retention period is required, so it cannot become "as long as needed").
- **Delete when nothing requires it.** A sentence no law requires, and no commercial deal needs, goes.
  A reservation ("we may ...") can replace a promise ("we will ...") where the business wants to keep
  the freedom without the duty.
- **Name the legal standard, not a specification.** "Appropriate technical and organisational
  measures" is the GDPR's own phrase. "AES-256 at rest, TLS 1.3, annual pen tests" is a spec the
  business then owes.
- **Make it true.** A rewrite has to describe what the system actually does today (Phase 3). The floor
  sets the ceiling of the promise. The facts set what can be said at all.

## Privacy policy

| Over-promise (lead) | Why it is over or untrue | Floor (verify) | Bounded rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "We will never share your data with anyone." | Untrue as soon as any processor exists (hosting, email, payments). | Disclose recipients or categories of recipients (GDPR Art 13(1)(e)). | "We share personal data with the recipients, or categories of recipients, listed in [section]." The list must cover every recipient in the inventory: processors, independent controllers (for example a payment provider acting as controller), joint controllers (for example an ad or social platform whose pixel or plugin collects and transmits visitors' data, CJEU C-40/17 *Fashion ID*, for which the essence of the joint-controller arrangement must also be made available, Art 26(2)), and authorities. |
| "We never sell your data." | Under the CCPA, "sell" and "share" include giving data to third parties for cross-context behavioural advertising (for example ad pixels). Untrue if such tags run, or if any vendor receives personal information without a CCPA service-provider or contractor contract. | If the CCPA applies and you sell or share: disclose it and offer the opt-out (§1798.135). | True and the CCPA applies: keep it, at the statute's floor, stated prominently (§1798.130(a)(5)(C)(i); 11 CCR §7011(e)(1)(D), §7013(g)): "We have not sold or shared personal information, as the CCPA defines those terms, in the preceding 12 months, and we do not sell or share it." If not true: disclose and offer the opt-out. |
| "We respond to every request within 7 days." | Shorter than the statutory deadline. | GDPR: without undue delay, at most one month, extendable by two (Art 12(3)). CCPA: 45 days, +45, for know/delete/correct (§1798.130(a)(2)); 15 business days for opt-out/limit (11 CCR §7026(f), §7027(g)). UK: Art 12A's start and pause rules. | "We respond within the time limits set by the data protection law that applies to your request." Or state the statutory periods themselves. |
| "You can have all your data deleted at any time." | Ignores the Art 17(3) exceptions, such as legal retention duties (tax and accounting) and legal claims. | Erasure on the Art 17(1) grounds, subject to Art 17(3). | "You can ask us to delete your personal data. We will do so unless the law requires or allows us to keep it, for example to meet tax or accounting obligations." |
| "We delete your data immediately when you close your account." | "Immediately" is an absolute, and backups and logs keep data for longer. | A retention period or its criteria (Art 13(2)(a)); storage limitation (Art 5(1)(e)). | "We delete your account data within [N] days of closure, and from backups within [M] days. We keep [category, e.g. invoices] for [period or criterion] because [law, e.g. tax law] requires it." N, M, and each longer period are what the system actually does (Phase 3), plus the operational margin it needs. |
| "We will notify you within 72 hours of any data breach." | The 72 hours apply to the **authority**, not to people. People are told only of breaches likely to result in a **high** risk. | Art 33(1) (to the authority); Art 34 (to the people affected). | Delete it, unless a regime that applies requires a breach statement (for example HIPAA, 45 CFR 164.520(b)(1)(v)(A), in a covered entity's notice of privacy practices): then state it in that regulation's words, with no period. Or: "Where the law requires us to inform you of a personal data breach, we will do so without undue delay." |
| "Your data is 100% secure" / "bank-grade" / "military-grade encryption". | An absolute and a specification. Under FTC Act §5, a security claim the business does not keep is deceptive. | Art 32: appropriate measures. There is no duty to describe them. | Delete it, unless a regime that applies requires a security statement (for example GLBA, 12 CFR 1016.6(a)(8)): then state the policies in general terms, as 1016.6(c)(6) allows. In a DPA, keep its description of the measures specific (`audit.md` 5A exception: EDPB Guidelines 07/2020 para 126; SCC Annex II) and check it only for truth. |
| "We encrypt all data at rest with AES-256 and use TLS 1.3." | A specification the business then owes, everywhere, forever. Often untrue for some store (logs, a third-party tool). | Art 32. | Delete it - except in a DPA, whose description of the measures must stay specific (EDPB Guidelines 07/2020 para 126; Decision 2021/914 Annex II; 2021/915 Annex III): check it only for truth. |
| "We honour Do Not Track signals." | CalOPPA only requires **disclosing** how DNT is handled, and only if the operator itself tracks users across third-party sites over time. Usually untrue. | Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §22575(b)(5). | If the operator tracks across third-party sites: "Our services do not respond to Do Not Track signals." (If true.) If it does not: nothing is required - delete it. |
| "We honour Global Privacy Control." | Processing signals is required only when the CCPA applies and the business sells or shares (11 CCR §7025(b)); similar duties exist under some other state laws. But explaining how signals are processed is required in every CCPA privacy policy (11 CCR §7011(e)(3)(F)). | Look up per regime. | Required to process: keep it, with the statute's scope. CCPA applies but no sale or sharing: replace the promise with a true §7011(e)(3)(F) explanation, for example "We do not sell or share personal information, so an opt-out preference signal such as Global Privacy Control does not change how we process your data. You can send an opt-out preference signal by turning on Global Privacy Control in a browser or browser extension that supports it." No regime requires it: delete it. |
| "Your data never leaves the EU." | A localisation promise the GDPR does not require. Usually untrue (support tools, email, error tracking, US-owned providers). | Disclose transfers and their safeguards (Art 13(1)(f)). | "Some of our service providers process personal data outside the EEA. Where they do, we rely on [the Commission's adequacy decision for that country / standard contractual clauses, a copy of which you can obtain from [contact or link]]." |
| "We anonymise all analytics data." | "Anonymous" in GDPR terms is a high bar (Recital 26). Hashed or pseudonymous IDs are still personal data. | Describe the processing truthfully. | "We use [tool] to collect usage statistics, including [data]." Use the word "anonymous" only where the data is truly anonymous. |
| "We will email you 30 days before any change to this policy." | No statute sets a fixed advance-notice period for privacy-notice updates. A new purpose must be notified before processing for it starts (Art 13(3)). | Art 13(3); accuracy and transparency (Art 12). | Delete it. Where CalOPPA applies, state how users are told of material changes and the effective date (§22575(b)(3)-(4)), as the system actually does it. Art 13(3) requires telling people before processing for a new purpose; it does not require a policy sentence promising so. |
| "We will never use your data for any other purpose." | An absolute. Further compatible processing is lawful under Art 6(4) conditions, and the law requires information first (Art 13(3)). | Art 6(4), Art 13(3). | Delete it. The purposes and legal bases stated elsewhere (Art 13(1)(c)) are what the law requires. Art 13(3) requires informing people before processing for a new purpose, not a policy sentence promising so. |
| "We have appointed a DPO" (when not required, or not true). | A DPO who is appointed takes on statutory tasks and protections (Arts 37-39). Naming one is a commitment. | Art 37(1), and national law under Art 37(4) (for example DE BDSG §38). | If not mandatory and not appointed: name a privacy contact instead ("Questions about privacy: [email]"). If a DPO IS appointed, mandatory or not, keep the DPO's contact details (Arts 13(1)(b), 37(7)) - un-appointing is a system-side decision, never a text trim. |
| A complaint right that names only one authority as the one to go to. | Misstates Art 77: people may complain to the authority where they live, work, or where the infringement happened. | Art 77(1); Art 13(2)(d). | "You have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, in particular in the EU or EEA country where you live or work, or where you believe the infringement took place." UK: "If you think our use of your personal data infringes data protection law, you have the right to complain to us at [contact]. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commission." (UK GDPR Art 13(2)(ca), (d); DPA 2018 ss164A, 165) |

## Terms of service

| Over-promise (lead) | Why it is over or untrue | Floor (verify) | Bounded rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "99.9% uptime" / any SLA in terms for everyone. | A guarantee no consumer or business law requires. | None, unless the service is sold with an SLA as part of the deal (CORE). | Delete it. Where paid plans include an SLA, keep it only in the plan's own SLA document, with its remedies and exclusions. |
| "Support responds within 24 hours." | A response time no law requires. | None, except platform rules such as P2B internal complaint handling (Art 11), which has no fixed hours. | "You can contact support at [channel]." |
| "30-day money-back guarantee, no questions asked." | A commercial guarantee is voluntary (SGD Art 17). It sits above the 14-day withdrawal right. | Consumer withdrawal: 14 days (CRD Art 9), plus statutory conformity remedies. | Delete it. In consumer terms, state the statutory withdrawal right if it is not already stated; in business-only terms, add nothing. Keep it only if it is part of the deal sold (CORE), recorded in `accepted.md`. |
| "No refunds under any circumstances." | **Below** the floor for consumers: the withdrawal right and the conformity remedies cannot be waived. | CRD Arts 9-16; DCD Art 22; SGD Art 21; CRA 2015 (UK). | "Payments are non-refundable, except where the law gives you a right to cancel or to a remedy, such as the 14-day right of withdrawal for consumers in the EU [with the conditions]." |
| "Your withdrawal right ends when you start using the service." | For a service (including a digital service such as SaaS), starting use never ends the right: it ends only once the service is **fully performed**, and for paid contracts only if performance began with the consumer's prior express consent and acknowledgement (Art 16(a)). For digital content not supplied on a tangible medium, it ends when supply begins, and for paid contracts only with prior express consent, acknowledgement, **and** the Art 8(7) confirmation (Art 16(m)). Otherwise the right stands, and a consumer who was not informed gets up to 12 more months. | CRD Art 16(a)/(m), Art 14(3)-(4), Art 10. | Make the text match the product type and the flow. Service the flow records an express request to start early for: state the 14-day right, and that a proportionate amount is owed for what was provided before withdrawal (Art 14(3)); if the request also carries the acknowledgement, add that the right ends once the service is fully performed (Art 16(a)). Service with no recorded request: state the 14-day right only - nothing is owed for the service provided during the withdrawal period (Art 14(4)(a)). Digital content: state the Art 16(m) exception only if the flow collects all three of its conditions. |
| "We will give you 30 days' notice of any change." | Longer than any general floor. The test for consumer terms is fairness, not a fixed period. | 93/13 Annex 1(j)/(k), DCD Art 19; fixed periods only in sector rules (P2B at least 15 days, longer where business users need time to adapt; EECC one month for non-app communications services; US state auto-renewal laws, e.g. Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code §17602(g)(2): fee changes 7-30 days before); UK: CRA 2015 Sch 2 paras 11, 13, 14, 15, 23, 25. | EU/UK consumer terms: "We will tell you about a change to your disadvantage reasonably in advance, by [channel], and you may terminate before it takes effect, unless the law requires the change to take effect sooner." Where the contract lets the business change its prices - EU: "We change our prices only for the reasons, and by the method, set out in [section]." (93/13 Annex 1(l), 2(d); CJEU C-92/11 *RWE Vertrieb*, operative part 2, which applies to contracts of indefinite duration too). UK: for a contract of indeterminate duration, CRA 2015 Sch 2 para 23 takes para 14 (price set after the consumer is bound) off the grey list when the trader must give reasonable notice and the consumer is free to dissolve the contract, and that right to cancel also meets para 15 - so the notice-and-terminate sentence is the statutory starting point; verify in `law.md` whether CRA s62/s68 still require a reasons-and-method clause before adding one. For fixed-term UK contracts, paras 14 and 15 apply, with the para 25 indexation exception. For other changes to the terms: a fixed-term consumer contract also needs "We change these terms only for the reasons set out in [section]." (Annex 1(j)); a contract of indeterminate duration needs only the notice and the right to terminate (Annex 2(b), second subparagraph; UK CRA 2015 Sch 2 para 23) - but a term letting the business change the characteristics of the goods or a non-digital service also needs "We change [the goods/service] only where we have a valid reason." (Annex 1(k), which unlike 1(j) does not require the reason to be specified in the contract; 2(b) does not cover it) - write a list of reasons only where the national law of a market that applies requires one, verified in `law.md`. In UK consumer contracts the same valid-reason sentence is needed for digital content and digital services too: CRA 2015 Sch 2 para 13 covers "goods, digital content or services", para 23 does not cover para 13, and the DCD Art 19 sentence below does not apply in the UK, unless the national law of a market that applies requires specified reasons - verify that in `law.md` before adding a reasons list. For consumer contracts for digital content or digital services, a change beyond what is needed to keep it in conformity with the contract is made only for the reasons in [section]; if it negatively affects your access to or use of it more than in a minor way, we will tell you reasonably in advance on a durable medium, and you may terminate the contract free of charge within 30 days of receiving our notice or of the change, whichever is later (DCD Art 19(1)(a), (d), 19(2)) - not needed if you can keep the unmodified version at no extra cost (Art 19(4)). US-only consumer terms: delete the period and keep a reservation: "We may change these terms. We will tell you about material changes before they take effect." (notice is what lets a change bind existing users); for automatic-renewal or continuous-service subscriptions sold to California consumers the business must also send the §17602(g) notices (fee changes 7-30 days before, with how to cancel), which the text need not promise. Business-only terms: delete the period and add nothing, unless a sector rule (P2B) applies; then use that rule's period. |
| "We may change these terms at any time without notice; continued use means acceptance." | **Below** the floor for consumers: unilateral change without a valid reason, notice, and a right to terminate is likely unfair. | 93/13 Annex 1(j); national lists; DCD Art 19; UK: CRA 2015 Sch 2 paras 11, 13, 14, 15, 23, 25. | State the valid reasons, give notice, and give a right to terminate (the rewrite in the row above). |
| "We are not liable for anything" / a total exclusion. | **Void** toward consumers, at least for death or personal injury caused by negligence (and, in several states, gross negligence and intent). In the UK the death/personal-injury rule binds business terms too (UCTA 1977 s2(1)), and other negligence exclusions there must be reasonable (s2(2)). | 93/13 Annex 1(a); UK CRA s65 (consumer), UCTA 1977 s2 (business); DE BGB §309 no. 7; national law. | "Nothing in these terms limits liability that cannot be limited by law." Then the limitation, within what the law allows. |
| "Governed exclusively by the laws of [X]; exclusive jurisdiction of the courts of [Y]" (B2C). | It hides the consumer's mandatory home-country protection (C-191/15; Rome I Art 6(2)) and home forum (Brussels Ia Arts 17-19). | Rome I Art 6(2); Brussels Ia Arts 17-19. | "These terms are governed by the laws of [X]. If you are a consumer, this choice does not deprive you of the protection of the mandatory provisions of the law of your country of residence where that law would apply to our contract without this choice, or of any right you have under the law to bring proceedings in the courts where you live." |
| "Lifetime access" / "free forever" / "unlimited". | An unbounded promise. "Lifetime" of what? It binds the business for as long as the product exists, and possibly longer. | None. | Delete it. Or bound it: "for as long as we offer the service, subject to these terms." |
| "We back up your data daily and can restore it on request." | An operational promise no law requires. | None (Art 32 is a measure, not a promise). | Delete it. |
| A link to the EU ODR platform. | The platform was discontinued, and the link duty ended on 20 July 2025. | Regulation (EU) 2024/3228. | Delete it. Name an ADR body only if you are committed or obliged to use one. |

## DPA (processor terms)

| Over-promise (lead) | Why it is over | Floor (verify) | Bounded rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "We will notify you of a personal data breach within 24 (or 48) hours." | The GDPR requires "without undue delay". | Art 33(2). | "We will notify you without undue delay after becoming aware of a personal data breach affecting your personal data." |
| "We will give 30 days' notice before adding a sub-processor." | The GDPR sets no period, only information and an opportunity to object. | Art 28(2); SCCs Clause 9 leaves the period to the parties. | "We will inform you of any intended addition or replacement of sub-processors [by channel], giving you the opportunity to object [within a reasonable period / N days, as agreed]." Where this sentence fills SCC Clause 9(a) Option 2, keep a specific period ("at least N days in advance"). |
| "You may audit us on-site, at any time, at our expense." | Art 28(3)(h) requires allowing for and contributing to audits, including inspections. It sets no cost allocation, frequency, or "any time". | Art 28(3)(h). | "We make available the information necessary to demonstrate compliance with Art 28, and allow for and contribute to audits, including inspections, by you or an auditor you mandate, on reasonable notice. [Cost and frequency terms, as agreed.]" |
| "We will delete all data within 7 days of termination." | The GDPR requires deletion or return at the end of the services, at the controller's choice. No days are fixed. | Art 28(3)(g). | "At the end of the services we will, at your choice, delete or return the personal data, and delete existing copies, unless Union or member-state law requires us to keep them." Add a period only if the system meets it, with margin for backups. |
| "We are ISO 27001 / SOC 2 certified." | A fact that must be true and current. If true, it is not a promise to stay certified unless worded as one. | None. | Delete it. Keep it only if the user records it in `accepted.md` (for example a customer contract requires it), and then only if true: "As of [date], we hold [certification]." Never "we will maintain". Not for a DPF self-certification: its adherence statement and Notice items are required (`law-floors.md`, United States). |
| "Unlimited liability for data protection breaches." | Not required by the GDPR. Art 82 governs liability toward data subjects; the allocation between the parties is contractual. | Art 82. | Agree it commercially. Do not volunteer it in a standard DPA. |

## Cookie policy and banner

| Over-promise or untrue (lead) | Why | Floor (verify) | Bounded rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "We only use essential cookies." | Untrue if analytics, ads, or embeds set cookies or read storage. | ePrivacy Art 5(3). | Make it true: describe strictly necessary storage as a category; for each consent-based cookie give its purpose, its duration, and whether third parties can access it, and gate it on consent (UK: or meet the Schedule A1 conditions, where they apply). Gating is a system-side fix. |
| "We do not use cookies." | Almost always untrue (session, CSRF, or framework storage). | ePrivacy Art 5(3). | "We use cookies and similar technologies that are strictly necessary to provide the service. [Others, if any, with consent.]" |
| Consent-based cookies listed without their duration or third-party access. | Mandatory information for consent cookies (*Planet49*). | CJEU C-673/17. | Add the duration, and whether third parties can access it, for each consent-based cookie. |

## Satellite copy

| Claim | Risk | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "GDPR compliant" / "fully compliant" | An absolute nobody can certify. Misleading if any finding exists. | Delete it. If a pointer is needed: "See our privacy policy." |
| "Cancel anytime" | A promise. It must match the terms (notice periods, prorating, refunds) and the cancel flow. | Make the terms and the flow agree, or bound the claim ("Cancel anytime; your plan stays active until the end of the paid period"). |
| "End-to-end encrypted" | A precise technical claim, which is false if the server can read the content. | Use it only if true in the strict sense. Otherwise delete it. |
| "We will never show you ads" | An absolute about future business model. | Delete it. |
| "2-year warranty included" / "14-day returns" offered as a perk / "climate-neutral" (via offsets) / "verified reviews" | Banned outright under UCPD Annex I pts 10, 4c, 23b (the last unless reasonable checks exist), even when true. UK: DMCCA 2024 Sch 20 para 11 covers "14-day returns" (the statutory cancellation right) and any "warranty" that gives no more than the CRA 2015 rights; para 13(3) covers reviews. The UK has no offsetting ban and no statutory 2-year guarantee: a genuine UK commercial guarantee is lawful, is checked under 5A/CORE, and must meet CRA 2015 s30(4) (plain language, and a statement that the consumer's statutory rights are not affected). | Delete it. (UK, genuine commercial guarantee: not banned - treat it as a COMMITMENT or CORE and check it for s30(4).) A statutory right may be stated as a right in the terms, never advertised as a feature of the offer. |
