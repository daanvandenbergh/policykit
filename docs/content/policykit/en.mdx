The package ships a Claude Code skill with two modes. **Author** writes a policy revision end to
end: it decides material-change-vs-erratum, creates the dated revision directory with one MDX file
per locale, sets the notice tier and the effective date, and validates. It is the [writing a
revision](/writing-a-revision/) checklist, executed. **Audit** reviews every policy you have,
clause by clause, against the law and against what your code actually does, and fixes what it
finds.

```text
/policykit [policy slug or description of the change - omit to be asked]
/policykit audit [policy slug] [--scan] [--out <path>] [--refresh-law]
```

It is user-invokable, so you can call it by name. It also triggers on its own when you ask for
the work in plain language: "add a policy revision", "update the terms", "publish the privacy
policy change", "fix a typo in a policy", "when does the new DPA take effect?", "audit our
privacy policy", "are we over-promising?", or "does the privacy policy match what the app does?".

Both modes draft to one doctrine: **promise exactly what the law requires, nothing more.** Every
sentence in a policy is an obligation you must keep for as long as the text stands, and can only
withdraw with a new revision, a notice, and sometimes fresh consent. So the skill writes the floor
the governing law sets, checked against the law's own text rather than recalled, and adds nothing on
top.

## Install it

You need the package installed first (see [getting started](/getting-started/)) - the skill and the
rules file ship inside it. Symlink the skill out of `node_modules` rather than copying it, so it
tracks the version of policykit you actually have:

```bash
mkdir -p .claude/skills
ln -s ../../node_modules/@daanvandenbergh/policykit/skills/policykit .claude/skills/policykit
```

Then add this line to your `CLAUDE.md` so the editing rules load into every session, not only the
ones that invoke the skill:

```text
@node_modules/@daanvandenbergh/policykit/rules/policies.md
```

## Author mode

Nine steps, in order.

0. **Locate the policy and its config.** It finds your `new Policy({ slug, dir, locales,
   defaultLocale? })` instances - conventionally one module such as `src/content/policies.ts` -
   and reads the target policy's `dir`, `locales` and `defaultLocale`. If you did not name a
   policy, it lists the configured slugs and asks.
1. **Material change or erratum?** An erratum is edited in place and the run stops after
   validation. A material change continues as a new revision.
2. **Pick the revision date.** Today as the UTC day (`date -u +%F`, the clock policykit binds on),
   zero-padded `YYYY-MM-DD`.
3. **Create the revision directory and write the text.** `<dir>/<date>/` with one `<locale>.mdx`
   per configured locale, matching `^[a-z]{2}(?:-[A-Z]{2})?\.mdx$`, each with a non-empty body -
   drafted to the legal minimum, never above it. Every period or right it states is checked
   against the law's own text, and every fact against your code.
4. **Write the frontmatter** on the default locale's file only: `effectiveFrom`, `notice` and
   `changeSummary`, plus an optional `title`. Other locale files get an optional `title` and
   nothing else.
5. **Set `effectiveFrom`.** A real calendar day on or after the revision date, far enough out to
   honour the notice window your current terms promise and any statutory notice floor.
6. **Self-audit the new text.** It runs the audit's clause checks and refutation pass (below) on
   the sentences the revision adds or changes, in every locale, checks the whole new revision for
   missing mandatory disclosures, and removes or weakens a sentence only once it has established that
   no applicable law requires it (otherwise it asks). It fixes what the checks confirm until no
   confirmed findings remain, or only ones blocked on you or fixable only in the system, which
   it lists for you. Findings in text carried over unchanged are listed for `/policykit audit`.
7. **Validate.** It runs your own test suite, which covers `assertValid()` / `assertValidAll()`,
   and fixes what throws until it is green - a full `next build` exercises the same loader. Every
   grammar or frontmatter breach throws a `PolicyValidationError` naming the file and field. See
   [validation](/validation/).
8. **Show the diff.** The git diff is the review artifact: one new dated directory (two when a
   next-day revision is needed), or one in-place erratum edit, and nothing else touched except the audit memory in
   `.agentstore/policykit/`. It reminds you that committed means published on the next deploy,
   that a revision must deploy no later than its revision date (a later deploy re-plans the change
   set for the deploy day first, so the notice window is not silently shortened), and that policy
   history is never rewritten.

## Audit mode

`/policykit audit` is built for the case where nobody else reviews your policies. It audits the
binding revision and every pending revision of every policy, in every locale. It also covers any other legal text
in the repo, and the promises your product copy makes outside the policies: "cancel anytime", "we
never sell your data", "GDPR compliant", cookie-banner and checkout text. Every clause gets three
checks:

- **True.** Each factual claim is checked against what the code actually does: processors, cookies,
  data collected, regions, retention, the consent flow. Business facts the code cannot show (your
  legal entity, your markets, your backup retention) are asked once, and stored. The policy is
  never treated as evidence of its own claims.
- **Lawful.** For each law that applies to your facts, it checks that every mandatory disclosure is
  present, that no term is void or unfair, and that no right is misstated. That includes outdated
  law, such as a link to the EU ODR platform, which shut down on 20 July 2025. Each requirement is
  verified against the primary source (EUR-Lex, legislation.gov.uk, the statute) and recorded with
  its URL and date, never taken from memory.
- **Minimum.** Every commitment is compared with the statutory floor. A promise the law does not
  require ("we respond within 7 days", "we notify you of any breach within 72 hours", "99.9%
  uptime", "your data never leaves the EU") is a finding, with a rewrite bounded at the floor. The
  check runs in both directions: it never trims a disclosure the law requires.

It also audits the versioning record. Is the notice tier right for what each revision changed? Does
each `effectiveFrom` honour the window the previous terms promised? Was published text edited in
place? Were translations copied forward?

Before anything is reported or fixed, fresh subagents try to refute each finding: a misread law, a
regime that does not apply, a clause read out of context, dead code taken as evidence, or a rewrite
that drops below the floor or creates a new promise. A separate cold read of each document then
looks for what the clause-by-clause pass missed.

**It fixes by default.** Confirmed findings with a text fix are applied. Errata are edited in
place. Any other fix to a managed policy goes into a new revision, written by the author mode's
rules. Product copy your terms do not incorporate and that is shown before a contract is made (marketing,
pricing, FAQ, signup text) binds only the customers who sign up while it is shown, so a trim there is
applied in place - it stops the promise reaching new customers -
and the old claim stays on the commitment register for customers who signed up while it was shown.
In other legal text, and in copy your terms incorporate by reference, corrections the binding change
clause or the law lets take effect at once are edited in place; a trim, or any other change that
owes notice, is reported with the notice it owes, not applied, because an in-place edit would skip
the notice window. Findings that rest on unverified law, open questions, promises you recorded as accepted, and system-side fixes are reported, never
applied. Trimming an over-promise reduces what customers get, so it carries at least `notify` and
the notice window your current terms promise. The new text is then audited again until no
confirmed findings remain, or only ones blocked on you or fixable only on the system side. It never
commits, pushes, or deploys, and never changes application logic - product copy that lives in code
is edited only as a string literal. When a policy states a fact your system does not match, it
changes the text to match the system and reports the system-side alternative. The exceptions are a
statement the law requires, a core term of the deal, and a commitment the policy makes: then the
text stays, and the finding is reported as a system-side fix or put to you as a question. When the
practice itself is unlawful, a truthful rewrite does not close the finding: it stays open
as a system-side fix.

| Flag | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `--scan` | Report only. No policy, page, or copy file is changed; the audit memory in `.agentstore/policykit/` is still updated. |
| `--out <path>` | Also save the report to `<path>`. |
| `--refresh-law` | Re-verify every cached legal source instead of reusing ones checked in the last 90 days. |

The report gives a verdict, which is never CLEAN while any regime, source, or fact is unverified. It
lists the findings with their legal citation and evidence, and the questions it needs you to answer.
It also gives the **commitment register**: every promise your policies still make, with its basis
(the law, the deal, or your recorded decision to keep it), and whether your system keeps it today.

The audit keeps its memory in `.agentstore/policykit/`: `facts.md` holds your confirmed business
facts, `law.md` the verified legal sources, and `accepted.md` the above-floor promises you decided
to keep on purpose. Commit the directory. It is how the next audit knows what was already settled.

Every report ends with the same statement: the review was done by an AI model, not a lawyer, it is
not legal advice, and it is least reliable wherever it says something was not verified.

## The judgement calls it makes for you

- **Material vs erratum.** Material means the meaning moved - a right, a price, a limit, an
  obligation, a data practice. If it has to ask, it is material.
- **The notice tier.** `none`, `notify` or `reconsent`, chosen from what the change does to the
  user, and recorded in frontmatter as the legal decision. Unsure means the stricter tier.
- **How much to promise.** It drafts to the legal floor - the statutory period, right or
  disclosure, and no better one - so you are not left keeping a promise the law never asked for.
  Where the governing law or the customer class is unsettled, it asks rather than defaulting to
  the generous reading.
- **One revision per day.** If today's directory is committed, the new revision is dated
  tomorrow. If it exists uncommitted and one `effectiveFrom` and tier serve both changes without
  shortening a notice window or delaying an immediate change, the change folds into it. Otherwise
  the immediate changes stay in today's directory and everything that owes notice moves to
  tomorrow's.
- **Fresh translations.** Every locale is translated from the new source text, never copied
  forward from the previous revision.
- **Supersession.** If the new revision supersedes one still inside its notice window, it carries
  the surviving changes, and the stricter tier, in a revision effective no earlier than the
  superseded one's `effectiveFrom` - a second revision dated the next day when the new one takes
  effect earlier.

Each of these is spelled out in full on [writing a revision](/writing-a-revision/), with the
layout grammar on [policy directories](/policy-directories/).

## The rules file

`rules/policies.md` is the other half of the contract. The skill reads it first and treats it as
binding - the steps are the workflow, the rules are the law. The `@`-import extends the same rules
to every agent session, including the ones that edit a policy without ever reaching for the skill.
Most of what it states is judgement no test can catch:

- A material change is a new dated directory, never an edit to a published one.
- A typo, format or link fix in a published file is an erratum: edit in place. If the fix changed
  what a clause means, it was never an erratum - revert and cut a revision.
- One revision per day; never amend a shipped revision.
- Promise the legal minimum and never more: the statutory bound, not a better one; no volunteered
  guarantees; no unbounded absolutes like "never" or "immediately". Removing an over-promise you
  already published is itself a material change, not a quiet in-place edit.
- A less protective privacy change never reaches data already collected on notice alone: where US
  users are in scope it applies only to data collected after it takes effect, unless users give
  affirmative express consent; in the EU and UK a new purpose also needs its own legal basis and
  prior information. A notice banner is not consent.
- The floor comes from the primary legal source, never from memory - and a required disclosure is
  never cut on a guess. Text an app store, API provider, or customer contract requires is kept on
  your decision, never trimmed as "not legally required".
- Every fact a policy states must be true of the system, checked against the code, never against
  the policy itself.
- Every locale of a new revision is translated fresh; a new locale starts at the revision that
  introduces it and never back-fills history.
- `notice` is the legal decision, recorded per revision, and the tier drives the UI: a `none`
  revision is never announced by the banner and never queued, so if users should see it coming, it
  was never `none`. See [notices and consent](/notices-and-consent/).
- A revision that supersedes a still-pending one never cuts its notice short: surviving changes go
  in a revision effective no earlier than the superseded one's `effectiveFrom`, and that revision
  records the superseded tier when stricter. A revision effective after a pending one builds on the
  pending text.
- Jointly gated policies take reconsent in revision order: a `reconsent` that binds after another
  policy's `reconsent` with an equal or newer revision string never re-prompts anyone, so the
  earlier-binding one moves to the later `effectiveFrom` - or, if it is already committed, the new
  one is dated after it.
- `effectiveFrom` honours the notice window your own terms promise; the package only checks
  `>= revision`.
- `changeSummary` says what changed and why the tier is right - evidence, never user copy.
- Drafts never sit in the content directory. Use `drafts/` or a branch.
- Never rewrite git history over policy content.

## What it will not do

- **It never cuts a revision for an erratum.** A fake revision flips your consent gates and
  re-prompts every user over a comma.
- **It never puts anything but `<locale>.mdx` files in a revision directory** - no images, no
  notes. Work in progress that must be committed goes in `drafts/` at the policy root, never
  inside a revision directory and never as a dated directory.
- **It does not decide your notice window.** That comes from your own published change-notice
  clause and any statutory notice floor. Where the window is unclear, it asks you.
- **It does not write you a more generous policy than the law requires.** Anything above the
  statutory floor is an obligation you then have to meet, so it stays at the floor unless you
  record that you want to keep a promise on purpose.
- **It does not commit, push, or deploy, and it does not change your application logic** (copy
  that lives in code is edited only as a string literal). You review the diff. When a stated fact
  and the system disagree, it fixes the text and tells you what the system-side fix would be. Where
  the law requires the statement, or it is a core term or a commitment, it leaves the text and
  reports a system-side fix or asks you instead; where the practice itself is unlawful, the finding stays open as a
  system-side fix.
- **It is not a lawyer.** It reports what it could not verify instead of passing it, and every audit
  report says it is not legal advice.
